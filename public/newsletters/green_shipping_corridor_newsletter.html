Executive Summary
From Announcements to Realisation

The global green shipping corridor portfolio expanded 4× in three years — from 21 initiatives in 2022 to 84 active initiatives in 2025. The market moved from mostly announcements in 2022–2023 toward more structured targets and implementation planning in 2023–2024, and by 2025 the first corridors crossed into the Realisation stage — meaning actual vessels, infrastructure, and/or fuel plants were moving into construction or operation.

The biggest qualitative shift in 2025 was not the growth in count, but the fact that 4 corridors reached Realisation for the first time since reporting began. The main bottleneck has shifted from project launch to crossing the so-called feasibility wall — moving beyond studies and MoUs into committed capital and physical build-out.

Structural Challenge
The Feasibility Wall — 2025's Primary Bottleneck
⚠ The Feasibility Wall
The movement added 23 initiatives in 2023, 18 in 2024, and 25 in 2025 — showing continued expansion even as projects became harder to advance beyond feasibility. The primary challenge in 2025 had shifted from project launch to crossing from feasibility into committed capital and physical build-out.

The record count of 84 active initiatives masks a concentration risk: the majority remain in Exploration or Preparation stages. Only 4 corridors have crossed into Realisation. For marine fuel professionals, this means green corridor demand signals are still largely forward-looking — but the first real-world volumes are beginning to materialise.

Quick Takeaways
What This Means for Marine Fuel Professionals
1
The 4× growth in three years signals structural momentum, not hype
From 21 to 84 active initiatives across shipping lanes worldwide. The corridor model has become the default framework for organising green fuel demand signals at scale.
2
Market shifted from announcements to structured targets in 2023–2024
15 corridors set operational targets in 2023; 17 existing initiatives completed key activities in 2024. The pipeline is maturing — MoU-only projects are increasingly the minority.
3
2025's Realisation breakthrough is the most commercially significant signal
Four corridors entered Realisation — meaning physical assets are being built. For fuel suppliers, this is where offtake agreements and supply chain positions need to be in place.
4
The feasibility wall is the next frontier — not project count
With 84 initiatives but only 4 in Realisation, the bottleneck is capital commitment and infrastructure readiness, not ambition. Corridor partners who can bridge this gap will define the market.
Data Source & Methodology Note
All figures sourced from Global Maritime Forum / Getting to Zero Coalition Annual Progress Reports (2022–2025). Reports do not use perfectly identical wording each year — "announced," "recorded," and "active" are not strictly interchangeable across editions. The 2024 report snapshot was taken as of 30 October 2024. Totals should be read as report-date snapshots, not exact year-end counts.
