Framework Overview
MARPOL Annex VI — The IMO Air Emission Regime
Background
MARPOL Annex VI was adopted in 1997 and entered into force on 19 May 2005, establishing IMO's binding air-pollution framework for international shipping. It covers SOx, NOx, particulate matter, ozone-depleting substances, onboard incineration, and VOC emissions from tankers. For marine diesel main engines, the most operationally critical elements are Regulation 13 (NOx Tiers) and Regulation 14 (sulphur/ECA controls). A mandatory energy-efficiency chapter was added in 2011, covering EEXI and CII under the GHG/CO2e pillar.

Compliance Methods
● Low-sulphur fuel oil — Switch to VLSFO (0.50%) or ULSFO/LSMGO (0.10%) when entering ECAs. Most common fleet approach.
● Exhaust gas cleaning (scrubbers) — Open or closed-loop systems allow continued use of HSFO. Equivalent compliance method accepted by IMO.

LCA Framework Note
IMO's 2024 LCA Guidelines define Well-to-Wake = Well-to-Tank + Tank-to-Wake, covering CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O. IMO explicitly states that ambition checkpoints should account for WtW GHG emissions — so the "CO2e" metric is properly treated as a GHG/WtW bucket, not just stack CO₂.
Regulatory Briefs
What Else Annex VI Covers
NOx, SOx, and GHG/CO2e are the primary commercial focus, but Annex VI contains four additional regulatory areas that operators must not overlook.
PM
Particulate Matter in ECAs
IMO explicitly includes PM controls within ECA requirements. Low-sulphur fuel compliance delivers PM co-benefits; scrubber operators in open-loop mode face port-state scrutiny over washwater discharge in certain ECAs.
ODS
Ozone-Depleting Substances — Deliberate Emissions Prohibited
Annex VI bans deliberate emissions of ozone-depleting substances from ships. Relevant to refrigeration plant and fire-suppression systems that may contain halons or HCFCs. Record-keeping required.
VOC
VOC Emissions from Tankers
Regulation 15 requires tankers in designated VOC ECAs to carry and operate an approved Vapour Emission Control System (VECS). Port states within those areas may specify requirements for loading operations.
INCIN
Shipboard Incineration Regulation
Regulation 16 governs the operation of shipboard incinerators. Incineration is prohibited in ports, harbours, and estuaries. Certain waste categories — PCBs, heavy metal residues, refined petroleum products — may never be incinerated at sea.
