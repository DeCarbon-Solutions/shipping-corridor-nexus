Executive Brief

MEPC 84 (27 April – 1 May 2026, chaired by Dr. Harry Conway) is not an adoption session — but it is the last regular MEPC before the November 2026 extraordinary session at which the IMO Net-Zero Framework (NZF) either lives, dies, or is fundamentally redesigned. With the US demanding termination, hydrocarbon states seeking abandonment, Japan proposing a watered-down middle path, and climate-vulnerable SIDS threatening to demand a stronger levy if negotiations reopen, MEPC 84 will filter which track survives — and signal to markets, investors, and regulators whether the IMO framework or the EU's unilateral ETS becomes the de facto global standard.

01 Why MEPC 84 Is a Hinge Point

The 84th session of the Marine Environment Protection Committee sits at the intersection of two failed processes: the MEPC 83 NZF approval (April 2025) that was then blocked at the MEPC/ES.2 extraordinary session (October 2025). MEPC 84 now serves as the procedural reset — deciding not just what happens to the NZF but whether IMO retains the credibility to govern shipping decarbonisation at all.

With 57 GHG documents before the committee — an abnormally heavy load reflecting acute political polarisation — the session precedes ISWG-GHG 21 (20–24 April), whose report feeds directly into deliberations. The key political question on agenda item 14: how and when to resume MEPC/ES.2, and on what terms.

02 The Three-Way Split: NZF Options Before MEPC 84

MEPC 84 faces a stark three-option landscape. Crucially, it will not adopt any of these positions — it will determine which family of options proceeds to the November 2026 extraordinary session. The European Parliament's own briefing warns that "no visible way ahead or convergence is yet ascertained."

US Position (MEPC 84/7/41): The most radical submission before the committee. Washington calls for ending the NZF entirely, requiring mandatory withdrawal of the EU ETS, adopting an "energy-all" approach that does not restrict any fuel type, and using the explicit acceptance procedure for any future amendments. Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned: "If the NZF or a substantially similar measure were to be brought back up at the IMO, our coalition will be ready to oppose, and it will be larger."

07 Implications for Industry & CJ Energy Solutions Clients

MEPC 84 is simultaneously a political filtering event and a technical drafting milestone. For shipowners, charterers, fuel suppliers, and financiers, the session's outcome shapes investment calculus across the entire 2028–2040 window.

  • Fleet renewal: Absence of NZF clarity increases the cost of capital for green-fuel-capable newbuilds. Owners holding methanol, ammonia, or hydrogen-ready vessels will face demand-side uncertainty until the GFI signal is locked in.
  • Fuel procurement strategy: The LCA framework outcome at MEPC 84 will determine whether bio-LNG and biofuels receive credits that make them economically competitive compliance tools under the NZF — or whether methane-slip and ILUC penalties negate their benefit.
  • Scrubber economics: If MEPC 84 signals movement toward Option B or C (weakened or no NZF), the VLSFO–HSFO spread dynamic changes. Scrubber investment returns improve if the IMO pricing signal is removed, but worsen if EU ETS alone drives compliance cost.
  • Carbon market exposure: Under Path C (termination), the EU ETS becomes the de facto global standard for European trades. Operators should model the scenario where EU ETS allowance prices absorb the full decarbonisation cost load — potentially significantly above current forward prices.
  • OCCS and wind propulsion: Guidelines on Onboard Carbon Capture (OCCS) and EEDI/EEXI methods for wind propulsion could create compliance pathways that don't require immediate fuel switching — relevant for operators managing older fleets through the transition period.
  • LNG fleet operators: MEPC 84's CH₄ and N₂O measurement guidelines will define how methane slip is measured and credited (or penalised). With 900+ LNG-fuelled vessels in operation and 500+ on order, this is one of the session's most commercially consequential technical outputs.

If the US successfully blocks even procedural progress at MEPC 84, the framework enters regulatory limbo — and the EU ETS becomes the de facto global standard by default, creating a split regime between European and rest-of-world trades. This bifurcation scenario, once theoretical, must now be treated as a base case for planning purposes by any operator with significant European trade exposure.
